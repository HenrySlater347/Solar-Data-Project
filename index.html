The Economics

Solar didn't get popular.
It got cheap.

In 2010, building a solar farm cost more than four times as much as a coal plant, per unit of electricity. By 2024 it was the cheapest way to generate power almost anywhere on Earth.

Based on the ~17,400 plants in the Global Power Plant Database (of 34,936 total) with a known commissioning year. Capacity is cumulative — each year adds that year's new plants on top of everything already standing.
Scale

1,865 GW installed worldwide, end of 2024

Up from 710 GW at the end of 2020 — solar PV capacity roughly two-and-a-half times over in four years, with 451 GW added in 2024 alone, more than any other power source.

Competitiveness

41% cheaper than fossil fuels, on average

By 2024, newly built solar was cheaper than the cheapest new fossil-fuel plant in the large majority of markets IRENA tracks — a full reversal from 2010, when it was over four times the price.

Four places to push

What actually moves the build-out curve

01

Speed up interconnection

The biggest bottleneck for new solar and wind isn't cost anymore — it's the queue to connect to the grid. Support permitting reform and transmission investment locally and nationally.

02

Choose a cleaner supplier

If your utility offers a renewable or green-tariff plan, switching sends real demand signals. Ask your provider directly what share of your power comes from solar and wind today.

03

Back storage, not just generation

Solar's cost collapse only pays off fully when batteries can shift that power to when it's needed. Storage costs have fallen even faster than solar — support policy that treats them as a pair.

04

Make the numbers visible

Most people still believe renewables cost more. Share what the data actually shows — with your city council, your workplace, your family — and let the economics do the arguing.

The one number that matters

Solar is already the cheapest power on Earth. The only thing still slow is us.

The grid took a century to build — and every year it isn't rebuilt faster is another year of paying more for dirtier power than we have to. The cost argument is over. Pick one of the four moves above, or put these numbers in front of someone who still thinks renewables cost extra. That's the actual bottleneck now.
