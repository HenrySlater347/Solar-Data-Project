Methodology & Sources

Everything on this site,
traced back to its source.

One primary dataset — 34,936 real power plants — plus the economic research that explains why solar's story changed. Every number you saw links back to where it came from.

Primary dataset

The plants themselves

Every dot on the map, every bar in the country and fuel-mix charts, and the entire build-out curve on the Collapse page come from this one dataset.

Core dataset

Global Power Plant Database, v1.3

An open, facility-level database of power plants worldwide — capacity, location, primary fuel, ownership, and (where known) commissioning year — built and maintained by the World Resources Institute with Google Earth Outreach, Global Energy Observatory, KTH Royal Institute of Technology, and Enipedia.

34,936 plants · 167 countries License: CC BY 4.0 Published by WRI
WRI project page WRI Open Data Portal GitHub repository
Used for: the world grid-map, fuel-mix and plant-count charts, the top-20 countries chart, the cumulative capacity build-out (stacked area), and every headline count/capacity stat on the site.
Economic research

Why solar got cheap

The cost figures on the Collapse page are external research, layered on top of the plant dataset — they explain the economics behind the build-out pattern the plants themselves show.

LCOE data

IRENA — Renewable Power Generation Costs

The International Renewable Energy Agency's annual benchmark report on the global weighted-average levelised cost of electricity (LCOE) for newly commissioned renewable plants, by technology and year of commissioning.

Editions used: 2018, 2020, 2023, 2024 Publisher: IRENA
2023 report 2024 report (PDF) IRENA solar overview IRENA cost data explorer
Used for: the $0.417 → $0.043/kWh LCOE curve, the 87% installed-cost decline, and the 1,865 GW / 451 GW deployment-scale stats.
Price history

Our World in Data — Solar Photovoltaic Panel Prices

A compiled long-run price series for solar modules, stitching together Nemet (2009) for 1975–2003, Farmer & Lafond (2016) for 2004–2009, and IRENA data from 2010 onward, inflation-adjusted to 2024 USD.

Coverage: 1975–2024 Publisher: Our World in Data (Oxford)

Used for: the $106/W (1976) → $0.38/W (2019) module-price comparison.

Caveats, in the open

Where the numbers get soft

  • Only about 17,400 of the 34,936 plants (roughly half) have a known commissioning year. Every chart built from commissioning year — the build-out curve, the fossil-vs-renewable share on The Shift — reflects that known subset, not the full database.
  • IRENA revises its historical LCOE figures as new project data comes in, so a given year's value can differ slightly between report editions. The figures here use the most recent edition available for each anchor year.
  • Module price and LCOE are two different measures (hardware cost vs. full cost of generated electricity) and are shown as two separate charts rather than mixed into one, to keep the comparison honest.
  • This is not investment, utility, or policy advice — it's a public dataset, made explorable.
